Three metres of wardrobe, as much open floor as possible

Your sketch, built in 3D with the new socket in the bottom-right corner, plus two layouts that keep the same storage and give you more empty floor and wall. Drag to orbit, pinch or scroll to zoom.

    Open floor counts floor that nothing stands on. Wall-mounted pieces count as open because you see floor underneath. Storage is gross wardrobe volume.

    Ideas that work in every layout

    New socket, bottom-right corner

    It sits inside the last wardrobe section whatever you choose. Fit it at skirting height and cut a hole in the Pax back panel. Use it for an LED strip on top of the wardrobe and a cordless vacuum or steamer that charges out of sight.

    Hinged doors, not sliding

    Pax with sliding doors is 66 cm deep, which cuts the walkway at the foot of the bed from 62 to 54 cm. Hinged 50 cm doors still open with 12 cm to spare and you only open one at a time.

    Build up, not along

    The 236 cm Pax frame holds about 17% more than the 201 cm one. That is the whole trick: same storage, shorter wardrobe, and no dusty gap above it. Measure the ceiling first; you need roughly 240 cm to stand the frames up.

    Make it disappear

    Handleless push-to-open doors in the same white as the walls. A wardrobe with no handles and no top gap reads as a wall, and the eye skips over it.

    Get things off the floor

    A wall-mounted desk and floating bedside shelf show floor underneath. The stool slides under the desk when you are not using it.

    Keep the middle empty

    One rug between the desk and the bed and nothing else. The wardrobe already shares the door wall, so you don't see it when you walk in.
